The Prince and the Carpenter's Ruler
In a certain kingdom, the people were as happy as larks in the morning. The good King ruled the land, but he had a very peculiar problem. His people had become so trusting that they would believe the sky was green if a stranger said so. This happened because the Devil made a bet with God. God allowed the Devil to whisper nonsense into the ears of the kingdom's people. 'A wide gate is a friendly gate,' the whisper would say, and so they took the gates off the city walls. Strangers arrived in droves, and soon the people wept bitterly. The strangers declared that soup should be eaten with a fork, so everyone in the kingdom did just that.
One day a stranger with shoes on his hands walked past the Queen and spat on her fine dress. The people clapped, for they thought it was a clever new custom. The next day, another stranger replaced the Queen's crown with a leaky bucket. The King's son saw this and was filled with a great sadness. On the third day, a stranger told the Queen her singing sounded like a rusty hinge, and he commanded her to be silent forever. The good Queen bowed her head and did not sing another note. The Prince then left the castle to find the sense his people had lost.
After many days on the road, he grew weary and hungry. He came upon a poor carpenter planing a piece of wood. 'You seek a heavy thing, my son,' said the carpenter. 'I seek the Good Sense that has fled from my kingdom,' answered the Prince. The carpenter gave him a small, simple ruler, and he said it would always measure truly. 'Do not look at what men say, but lay this ruler against what they do,' the carpenter commanded. So the Prince took the ruler and walked on.
At last he came to a crossroads where the road signs spun like weather vanes. He held up the carpenter's ruler to the signs. The signs stopped spinning, and one pointed down a dark path while the other pointed to a bright road. He took the bright road. On this road sat a great chasm, wide and deep. He took out a piece of chalk and drew a bridge upon the air, and because his heart was true, the bridge became solid.
Then he entered a village where everyone was trying to fill a well with a sieve. Their task was to fetch water for their thirsty children. The ruler in his hand grew warm and spoke in a clear voice, 'A lie makes a poor bucket.' The Prince went to the well and lowered a wooden bucket and drew up cool water. When the people saw how simple it was, they were glad and threw their sieves away.
And he walked on until he came to a field of men. They were planting coins in the dirt. They hoped to grow money trees, but they were sad because only weeds grew. Then the Prince remembered the carpenter's words and held up the ruler. As quick as lightning, the ruler showed them that good grain grows from honest seed. The Prince took a handful of wheat and planted it in the ground. 'Good bread comes from sweat, not from wishes,' said the ruler. The men then plowed the fields properly.
And he walked on farther, and there he found the tallest mountain in the world. Atop the mountain sat a group of very important men. They had fine clothes, long beards, and heavy books, and they argued about how many clouds could fit on the head of a pin. The Prince was not angry but felt a great pity for them.
Kneeling down, the Prince drew a simple door at the base of the mountain. He opened the little chalk door and walked straight through the mountain. The mountain then crumbled into dust. But the important men were left sitting on a cloud, for their arguments were made of air. A wisp of black smoke fled from the dust of the mountain. The Devil had lost his bet and could whisper no more. The land became clear again, like a window that has been washed clean.
And the Prince saw that the carpenter's ruler had turned into a simple walking stick. The roads were straight again, the wells were full of good water, and the fields were green with grain. A great peace fell over all the land. Beside a clear stream stood a maiden whose heart was true.
So the Prince and the maiden walked back to the castle. As they passed the city walls, the leaky bucket on the Queen's head shouted, 'Our work is undone!' Then all the strangers turned into smoke and were blown away by the wind. The Prince married the maiden, the Queen sang once more, and they all lived with Good Sense for the rest of their days.
