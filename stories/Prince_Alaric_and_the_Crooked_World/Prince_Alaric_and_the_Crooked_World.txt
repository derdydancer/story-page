Prince Alaric and the Crooked World
In a certain kingdom, the King was as kind as summer rain. But his Queen ruled the house, and her mother ruled the Queen. So all the men in that land were ruled by their wives, and everything was turned upside down. This happened because the Devil made a bet with God in Heaven. The Devil said, 'I can make any man forget his place,' but God knew His own. And God allowed the test, for He knew the King had a good son named Prince Alaric. The Prince was strong and pious, but he saw the foolishness and wept bitterly in his heart. He too was married to a princess who did not listen to him.
One day, the King's mother-in-law came to the castle and demanded the King’s crown to wear as a hat. The King was sad, but he gave it to her because the Queen told him to. Prince Alaric saw this and his spirit grew dark within him. The next day, his own mother the Queen told the King, 'Dear husband, your throne is too hard, so you must sit on the floor while I use it for my feet.' The King sat on the cold stones, and the Prince saw this fresh insult. On the third day, the Prince's own wife took his royal scepter to stir her soup. Then the Prince knew he must leave that place and find God’s true order.
So on the morrow, he took only a loaf of bread and a staff and walked out of the city gates. He knelt on the road and prayed to God for guidance. Then a small white bird landed on his staff and sang, 'Go east, go east, where the sun does rise, and set the crooked world to rights.' The Prince understood the command was from Heaven. He thanked God for the sign, and so he began to walk towards the east. A voice from the clouds then spoke, 'Do not suffer foolishness, nor allow the cart to pull the horse.' And he walked for a long time until he came to a dark forest.
After three days in the wood, he came upon a strange sight in a clearing. A man was pushing a wheelbarrow full of stones with his teeth. His wife sat upon the stones and she held a whip. The Prince stopped. Into the clearing he strode with a firm step. He took the whip from the woman and put the man in her place, for that was the proper way.
Next, he traveled until he reached a river that flowed uphill. Fish were tumbling out of the water and gasping on the banks. The Prince stood on the bank and spoke to the river, 'River, you flow to the sea! Go down, not up, as God has willed!' The river heard his voice and it turned around and it flowed down toward the great sea. When the fish were safe again in the water, they jumped for joy.
Then he walked on again and came to a field of weeping cabbages. A farmer was watering them with salty tears from a bucket. The farmer said his wife told him the cabbages were not sad enough, and so he must make them weep. Then the Prince took the farmer's bucket and filled it with fresh water from the newly-ordered river. He watered one cabbage, and as quick as lightning the whole field turned green and healthy. The farmer fell to his knees and thanked the Prince for his wisdom. Just then the farmer's wife came out of a cottage and said, 'You fool, you have ruined my sad soup!' The Prince pointed his staff at her and said nothing. And then a great wind came and it blew the woman away like a dry leaf, for she had made war on the natural order of things.
The Prince saw that his work here was done. The white bird returned to his shoulder, and the sun broke through the clouds, and all the world seemed right again. He was glad in his heart.
Turning homeward, he walked with a new strength in his stride. When he reached the castle, he found the gates were shut tight. His wife stood on the wall. But he looked at her and said, 'Open the gate, for the master of this house has returned.' She laughed and threw a stone down at him. The stone missed the Prince but struck the castle wall, and the wall turned to soup. His wife, the Queen, and the Queen's mother all fell into the soup wall and were boiled like so many foolish vegetables.
And the Prince walked into the castle over the firm ground where the wall had been. His father the King was now wearing his crown and sitting on his throne. All the people of the court were glad. In the great hall, a new chair appeared beside the King's throne. Then a door appeared on the far wall, and from it stepped a maiden. She curtsied to the King and then to the Prince and said, 'My lord, I have come as God has willed it, to be a true helpmeet.' Far below the earth, the Devil gnashed his teeth, for he had lost his bet. And so Prince Alaric and his new bride ruled wisely, and they all lived happily to the end of their days.
