The Prince and the Sinking Kingdom

In a certain kingdom, the people grew as round as apples in autumn. The King was a good king, but he was also a very wide king. Everyone ate all day, and so the very ground began to sink under their weight. A man could not tie his own shoes, for his belly was in the way. Only the King's son, the Prince, was still lean. Up in Heaven, God said to the Devil, 'See that Prince, he will not fall to this gluttony.' And the Devil answered, 'We shall see, for I will tempt him with all the delights of the world.' God agreed to the wager, for the Prince's heart was his own to keep or to lose.
Down below, a great sadness fell upon the Prince, so he knew he must leave the castle. His father the King wept bitterly. 'Do not go, my son,' said the King, 'Stay and have this nice cake with seven layers of cream.' 'I cannot, dear father,' said the Prince, 'for my heart is heavy as a stone.' He took a piece of chalk and drew a door on the wall, and the door opened for him. The Prince stepped through the door and onto a long, dusty road. Behind him, the door turned back into cold stone.
After a time, the road split in two. One path led to a wide, brown river that smelled of gravy. The other path was narrow and stony and went up a steep hill. The Prince's stomach rumbled like thunder. But he thought of the sinking kingdom, and he took the stony path up the hill. At the top of the hill sat an old hermit. The hermit gave the Prince a single piece of dry bread and a cup of cold water. The Prince ate the bread and drank the water, and his hunger went away. He walked on and soon came to a dark wood. In this wood, the trees did not grow leaves, but instead grew hot roasted chickens on every branch. A great hunger seized him once more. But he remembered the sad, wide face of his father. So he closed his eyes and walked straight through the wood without touching a single chicken.
At last, the Prince came to the bottom of a great, tall mountain. The mountain was made of smooth, slippery glass, and there was no path at all. The Prince tried to climb, but for every step he took, he slid back two, and at last he grew weary and knelt upon the ground to pray. As he prayed, a great stairway of pure white clouds lowered itself from the sky. He climbed the cloud-stairs all the way to the top of the glass mountain. From the top, he could see his whole kingdom. It had sunk so far into the ground that only the castle's tallest tower was showing. A great pity filled the Prince's heart, and he knew he must return. He slid down the other side of the mountain as if on a sheet of ice. In a moment he was back at the castle walls. He found his father, the King, wedged so tightly in his throne that he could not move. The Prince held out his hand and said, 'Father, do not eat the cake, eat only this.' From his pocket he pulled a dry crust of the hermit's bread. But just then, the Devil himself appeared in a puff of greasy smoke. He held a cake that looked like an angel's cloud, and it smelled of honey and dreams. The King's eyes grew wide, and he reached a trembling hand for the Devil's cake. But the Prince was quick and put the dry crust into the King's open mouth. The moment the King tasted the bread, he began to shrink. All through the kingdom, people shrank back to their proper size. The ground, now free of its great burden, rose up with a mighty groan. The Devil, seeing his prize lost, shrieked in anger. He vanished, leaving behind only the smell of burnt sugar. Up in Heaven, God smiled. The King was now as lean as a boy and stood up from his throne. He hugged his good son. 'From now on,' said the King, 'we shall eat only what we need, and no more.' And all the people of the kingdom cheered, for they felt light and happy. Now, every man could tie his own shoes. The Prince ruled wisely after his father. And the kingdom never sank again. They all lived happily to the end of their days. My tale is done, and see, there runs a mouse; whoever catches it may make a great fur cap of it.
