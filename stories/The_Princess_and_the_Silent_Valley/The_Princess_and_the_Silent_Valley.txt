The Princess and the Silent Valley
Once upon a time, in a kingdom far away, there was a king. The king had a young daughter named Angelina who lived in a tall castle. She had many fine dresses and jewels, but she suffered from a strange curse that kept her mouth always open. In her grand chambers, the windows were always left ajar, letting in the dust and the cold. The princess shared her warm bread with the little birds that flew inside through the open windows. She often thought, 'I wish I could close the gates of my own small castle.' She was a quiet and sad child. And so the years passed in this way. Then one day, a traveling physician arrived at the court. He looked upon the princess and said her ailment was that the doors to her spirit were left unguarded. The princess was filled with hope. 'Your cure lies in the Silent Valley,' said the physician, 'if you can find it.' He told her she must fetch a single drop of water from the soundless spring, and then she would be well. The princess agreed to make the journey. And so she set out from the castle the very next morning. After many days, at the edge of a dark wood, she met an old woman. The old woman was gathering fallen branches. 'Whither goest thou, my child?' asked the woman. 'I seek the Silent Valley to cure my affliction,' replied the princess honestly. The woman was pleased and gave the princess a small, smooth stone from the riverbed.
'Hold this stone upon your lips when the wind speaks,' the woman warned, 'and you shall not be led astray.' Then the princess thanked the old woman and went on her way. At last, she came to the Whispering Mountains, which were tall and grey. She held the smooth stone against her lips and the passage was quieted. The mountain winds howled with gossip and doubt, but the sounds could not enter her and cloud her thoughts. She walked on into the valley. There, by a still lake, sat a hermit before a dark cave. She approached him silently, the stone having taught her the wisdom of a closed mouth. Now the hermit set a task for her. He commanded her to sort a mountain of mixed seeds before the sun set. At this impossible task, the birds she had once fed came fluttering down from the sky. They picked and they sorted, and they separated the good seeds from the bad, and the task was done in an hour. When the hermit saw this, he was not pleased. And so he gave her a second task. She must fetch the golden cup from the bottom of the deep lake without making a single ripple. The princess was sad and did not know how to do this. But then she remembered the lesson of the stone, and she quieted her body and her breath. She entered the water so gently that the surface did not break, and she retrieved the cup in perfect stillness. The princess brought the golden cup to the hermit. 'You are lucky,' the hermit snarled, 'but you cannot pass the final test.'
The princess waited and said nothing at all. Then the hermit set the third task and said, 'You must now tell me my own true name.' This was the most difficult challenge of all, for no one knew the hermit's name. The princess closed her eyes and listened with the perfect stillness she had learned, and she heard only the sound of his own ragged, open-mouthed breathing. The hermit became enraged by her long silence. Leaping to his feet, he let out a great shout of fury. The shout echoed from the rock, and a great stone was dislodged from the roof of his cave. The princess was still and her mind was clear. But instead of running, she saw the shadow of the falling stone and took one silent step aside. She moved without making a sound. The great stone crashed down and sealed the entrance to the cave, and the hermit and his noise were trapped within forever. The valley fell into a profound silence, and on a single blade of grass, a drop of water appeared like a tear. And she collected the single drop of water in a vial. When the water touched her skin, a great calm filled her, and she drew her first quiet, clean breath through her nose. She was overcome with a gentle joy. Her reward was the quiet peace of a guarded spirit. And so she returned to the castle, no longer a sad girl but a wise woman. When she entered the great hall, the court fell silent, for her very presence commanded peace. The gossips and fools who had once made light of her affliction now held their tongues in her wake, their own mouths firmly closed. And they all lived in a more thoughtful quiet from that day forward.
