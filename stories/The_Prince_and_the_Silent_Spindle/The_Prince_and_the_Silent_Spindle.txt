The Prince and the Silent Spindle
In a certain kingdom, the bread was always flat and the fences were always broken. An old King ruled this land, and he had one good son. But a strange sickness was in the kingdom, for all the wives were contrary. A wife would plant stones when her husband said to plant corn, so their fields were barren. She would sweep dust into the house when he asked her to sweep it out, so their homes were dirty. The husbands would sigh and shake their heads, but the wives only laughed. This foolishness made everyone poor, and the whole kingdom was grey like ash. The King saw this and was filled with sorrow. He called his son to him and said, "You must take a wife and set a good example for the land." So the Prince went to the house of the first Duke, for the Duke had a daughter. The daughter was fair, but she said to the Prince, "I will not marry you, for your horse is brown and I only like horses the colour of the sky." The Prince then went to the second Duke, who also had a daughter. This daughter served him a pig with no trotters and said, "This is a special pig that walks on its knees." Finally, the Prince went to the third Duke, and this daughter poured his cup of wine onto her own shoes. She said, "My shoes were thirsty," and she giggled like a fool. The Prince rode back to the castle, and he was very angry. That night, he put on the simple clothes of a huntsman and took nothing but a piece of bread. "I will not find a wife," he said, "but I will find the root of this madness." He left the great castle and walked down the dusty road. He saw that all the cottages were dark, for the husbands were asleep. But from each cottage, a woman slipped out like a shadow. And they all went into the dark forest at the edge of the kingdom.
When they reached the forest, the Prince followed them quietly. He hid behind an old oak tree and watched. The women came to a large, grim cottage that had no windows, but a dull light shone from under the door. They entered one by one. Inside sat a small, hunched man with eyes like shiny pebbles. He was a miser, and he owned a hundred looms in that dark cottage. All the contrary wives sat at the looms and began to weave. They wove a strange grey cloth that seemed to drink the light from the room. The miser watched them and cackled, "Weave faster, my dears, weave faster!" In the center of the room, a single spindle turned on a stool, and it spun with no thread at all. When the Prince saw this spindle, he knew it was the source of all the trouble. Then he saw the miser give each woman a single polished pebble for her hard work. The women looked at the pebbles as if they were great treasures. The Prince was sad for them, but he knew he could not fight a hundred foolish women and a miser. Then he remembered what his old nurse once told him. He stepped from the shadows and walked into the center of the room. The women looked up from their looms, and the miser screeched like a bat. The Prince paid them no mind and said to the spindle, "Be still." "Your work is done." At once the spindle stopped its turning, and it was quiet. The grey cloth on every loom turned to dust. The women looked at the pebbles in their hands, and they saw they were just worthless stones. The miser shrieked with rage. Rushing forward, he tripped over the stool and fell into the great pile of grey dust. The dust covered him completely. All was silent. But then the women began to weep bitterly. They ran from the cottage without looking back. The Prince watched them go, and he knew they would be contrary no more. The air in the forest grew fresh and clean, as if a great storm had passed.
Then the Prince went back to the castle. The kingdom was changed, for the sun was bright and the fields were green. The people were glad, and they sang in the streets. Their bread was light and their fences were mended. And when the Prince walked into the castle garden, he saw a maiden sitting by the well. She was spinning thread as gold as the sun, and she said, "My Prince, your kingdom is whole again, and so I am here." The Prince took her hand, and they were married that very day. And they lived happily, and the wives of that kingdom were never contrary again.
