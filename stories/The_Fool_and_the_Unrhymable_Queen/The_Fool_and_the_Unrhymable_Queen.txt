The Fool and the Unrhymable Queen
Once upon a time, there was a kingdom. A sad queen ruled over the land. The kingdom had a grand castle and great wealth, but all the people were forced to speak in rhymes. In a small hut at the edge of the kingdom lived a fool. He spent his days polishing a single shoe and collecting silent moths in a jar. He thought, 'I wish to see things as they are.' The people were heavy of heart. And so it went for a long, long time. Then one day, a decree was made. A golden quill appeared in the castle courtyard. The queen was afraid. The queen said, 'Whosoever can make this quill write prose, I shall grant a wish.' The one who succeeds will have half the kingdom, and the one who fails will be turned to words. The fool agreed to the task. And so he set off for the castle. After a time, he came to a crossroads. There he met an old woman. The old woman said, 'Pray, what do you carry?' The fool said, 'I carry a broken lute, a single shoe, and a jar of silent moths.' The woman was pleased with his honesty, and she gave him a smooth, grey stone.
She said, 'Do not speak in rhyme when you hold this stone.' Then the fool thanked her and went on his way. At last, he came to a great wall of swirling words. He held the grey stone in his hand, and the words stopped moving. A small door appeared in the wall, and a path lay before him. He went inside. Before him stood two guards made of ringing bells. He held the stone tight, and the bells fell silent so he could pass. Then he faced the first trial of the Rhyming Queen. He had to cross a chasm by speaking a perfect couplet. From his jar flew the silent moths. One moth landed on this word and one moth landed on that word, and a bridge of silence formed across the void. When the queen saw this from her scrying pool, she was angry. And so the fool faced the second trial. He had to make a golem of song laugh. The fool could not think of a joke, and he did not know what to do. Then he remembered his broken lute. He played a note that was not a note, and in the blink of an eye, the golem shattered from the soundless sound. The fool walked into the throne room and stood before the queen. The queen hissed, 'You will never break my perfect verse, you witless thing!'
The fool simply held up his one shoe. Then the queen gave the third trial, and it was a riddle. She commanded him to find the one thing in her kingdom that had no rhyme. The silent moths swirled around his head, the soundless lute hummed in his hand, and the smooth stone grew warm in his pocket. The queen was filled with rage. Rising from her throne, the queen began to chant. The very air turned to rhyming couplets that bound the fool. The fool could not move. But he looked at the queen, and with the answer to the riddle, he spoke the one thing that had no rhyme. He said the queen's true name. The queen shattered into a thousand letters. The rhyming curse lifted like a mist, and the castle fell silent. Then the fool picked up the golden quill. From the scattered letters, a young and kind king formed. There was great joy in the land. The new king made the fool his chief advisor and gave him the other shoe. And the people could speak their minds freely once more. The golden quill wrote the truth of the curse for all to see. The bad rhymes were swept out of the castle and burned. And they all lived happily ever after.
