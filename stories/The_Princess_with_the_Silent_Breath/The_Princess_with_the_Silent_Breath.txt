The Princess with the Silent Breath
Once upon a time, there was a kingdom. A young princess named Angelina lived in the castle. She had many fine gowns and jewels, but she could not breathe through her nose. In the great stone castle, her mouth was always open like an unguarded gate. The princess offered bread to the birds in the courtyard. She often thought that her own thoughts flew away like those very birds. Her mind was always in a flutter. And so the days passed, one just like the other. Then one day, an old woman came to the castle. She was a bent and wrinkled woman who had cursed the princess at her birth.
The king was filled with anger. "You must find a cure for my daughter," said the king. "Find the cure and you shall have gold, or fail and you will be banished." Angelina promised she would try. And so she left the castle and went out into the world. After many days on the road, she came to a small cottage. She met a quiet old groundskeeper tending his garden. "Pray, where are you going with such a troubled look?" asked the groundskeeper. "I seek a cure for a terrible curse," Angelina replied. The groundskeeper saw her kindness and gave her a small, smooth river stone.
"Do not speak when this is in your mouth, and keep the gate of your lips closed upon it," he said. So the princess thanked him, took the stone, and went on her way. At last she came to a windy mountain pass. Angelina placed the stone in her mouth and closed her lips around it. The wind howled and dust swirled, but no cold air entered her lungs and her mind felt clear. She walked forward. Before her stood two great gusts of wind, like invisible guards. She held the stone in her mouth and walked between them, and they could not push her thoughts away. Finally, she found the old woman's hut. The old woman told her to separate a pile of scattered seeds from a pile of dust before nightfall.
Angelina sat down, and the birds she had fed in the courtyard flew to her side. They picked and sorted and pecked, and soon the seeds were in one pile and the dust was in another. When the old woman saw this, she grew angry. But she gave the princess a second task. She had to fetch water from a distant spring in a cup made of lace. The princess sat by the spring and did not know what to do. Then she remembered the groundskeeper's advice and put the stone in her mouth, and her mind became still and clear. In that quiet moment, she saw moss growing on a rock, so she lined the lace cup with the moss and it held the water perfectly. Angelina brought the water to the old woman. "You are clever, but your open mouth will always be your undoing," hissed the old woman.
The princess did not answer, but kept her lips closed upon the stone. Then the old woman gave her the third and final task. She must spend one night in a room where the walls whispered distracting lies. Angelina took her stone and listened to the birdsong from outside, and she did not open her mouth to the lies all night. The old woman was filled with fury, for all her tricks were spent. Rushing forward, the old woman tried to shout a final, binding word of the curse into the princess's mouth. She blew a foul and dusty wind from her own lungs at the princess. The princess nearly gasped and opened her mouth in fright. But at the last moment, she remembered to draw a breath through her nose, something she had never done before. A tiny, quiet stream of air flowed in, pure and clean.
The old woman's foul wind, having no open gate to enter, blew back upon her and she choked on her own dust and vanished. At that moment, the curse was broken like a snapped thread. And the princess held the simple stone in her hand as her prize. Her face was now calm and her expression was wise and serene. There was much joy when she returned. The king gave her half the kingdom to rule herself, for she had gained great wisdom. And Angelina returned to the castle a princess no more, but a queen. The people saw her quiet strength and knew her to be the true ruler. The memory of the dusty old woman was swept away from the land forever. And she ruled wisely and lived happily for all her days.
